# Studio Culinar-E

**Engineer Bold Compositions. Spread Culture.**

A flavor architecture tool by [Studio Monk-E](https://github.com/StudioMonk-E).

---

## What is this?

Studio Culinar-E treats ingredients as building materials. Instead of asking "what goes with what," it asks a harder question: *what connects two things that don't obviously belong together?*

Pick two ingredients. The studio drafts a **blueprint** — finding **keystones** (bridge ingredients) that structurally link them, ranked by bond strength. It also tells you when two ingredients already share a direct **foundation**, so you know when a bridge isn't even necessary.

But flavor is never just flavor.

334 ingredients. Symmetric pairing data. Cultural range from açaí to za'atar, mandioca to mole, dendê to dashi, egusi to doubanjiang. And underneath the pairings: **125 ingredients annotated with a cultural data layer** — where each ingredient originates, how colonialism and migration moved it across the world, what it carries politically and economically. So when the tool tells you that two ingredients connect through a keystone, it can also tell you what that keystone means — which trade routes it traveled, whose labor produced it, what it cost to get here.

This isn't a recipe tool. It's a research tool that treats food as a material system encoding history.

---

## Features

**The Blueprint** — Select two materials, choose a flavor palette, and draft a blueprint. The studio returns keystones ranked from structural (strong, iconic) to speculative (experimental, uncharted).

**Pantry Archive** — Explore a single ingredient: its type, taste profile, every known connection sorted by bond strength, and — for annotated ingredients — its full cultural and historical record.

**Cultural Data Layer** — 125 ingredients carry annotations on geographic origin, colonial trade routes, migration patterns, and labor and class histories. Expanding with each build.

## Part of Studio Monk-E

Studio Culinar-E is one project inside a larger practice. Studio Monk-E is a cultural material lab — researching how ingredients encode history, labor, migration, and class. The tools we build reflect that framework.

Culinar-E sits alongside ongoing research into food as a system: how commodities travel from extraction zone to breakfast bowl, what gets erased in that journey, and what a tool that remembers might look like.
